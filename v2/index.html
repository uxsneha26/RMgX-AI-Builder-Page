01 — Approach

Most briefs arrive tangled.
Good. That’s where we start.

ambiguousclear direction

Before we draw a single screen, we find out who the product is for, what’s really in their way, and which decision matters most. Often the request we’re handed and the problem worth solving are two different things.

  1. eShipper · Logistics

    Where it could have startedRedesign the shipping dashboard.

    What it really wasCustomers were walked through every shipment by an in-house team. The work was automation, marketplace integrations and an e-commerce journey that could scale on its own.

  2. Forces Horizon · Career transition

    Where it could have startedBuild an AI résumé builder.

    What it really wasVeterans couldn’t translate service records into language employers understand. They needed a step-by-step transition plan, not just a better CV.

  3. Decimal Factor · SME lending

    Where it could have startedMake loan approvals faster.

    What it really wasLending officers needed automation they could trust: every recommendation explainable, every decision trail auditable.

  • a.People before pixels. We talk to the people who’ll use it, then to the people who’ll pay for it.
  • b.Problems before features. The cheapest screen is the one you didn’t need to build.
  • c.Decisions before decoration. Every element earns its place, or it goes.
  • d.Engineers in the room. Feasibility is a design input, not a surprise in sprint six.
